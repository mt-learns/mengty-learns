## TL;DR
Good engineering decisions are **timely, evidence-based, and owned by one Approver**. Push **reversible** choices down to the engineer closest to the work, reserve **ADRs and DACI** for hard-to-reverse choices, and always separate **facts** from **assumptions** before picking an option.

## Why it exists
Teams stall in two opposite ways: **analysis paralysis** (everyone debates for three weeks because nobody knows who the single Approver is) or **unrecorded gut calls** (six months later nobody remembers why a risky database or vendor choice was made).

## Mental model: DACI + Reversibility
Atlassian's **DACI** framework prevents committee gridlock:
- **Driver (D):** Moves the decision forward—collects facts, drafts options, sets the deadline.
- **Approver (A):** **One person** with final sign-off authority.
- **Contributors (C):** Subject-matter experts (Security, Ops, QA, Product) whose input shapes the options.
- **Informed (I):** Stakeholders notified once the decision is made.

Pair DACI with **reversibility**: if a choice is easy to undo (e.g., an internal helper module), let the responsible developer decide in a PR. If it is costly to undo (e.g., public API contract, auth model, database engine), write a short **ADR** (`process/adr`).

## Do it: 8-step decision process

1. **Frame the problem & outcome:** What must be true after this decision?
2. **Separate facts from assumptions:** List verified metrics/constraints separately from guesses.
3. **List hard constraints:** Budget, deadline, compliance, latency, team skills.
4. **Draft 2–3 real options:** Include *"do nothing / defer"* when relevant.
5. **Compare trade-offs:** Value, delivery time, operational cost, risk, maintainability, and reversibility.
6. **Consult only essential experts:** Keep Contributors small (`process/raci`).
7. **Assign one Approver and decide:** Pick an owner and set a decision deadline.
8. **Log and revisit:** Document in an ADR/ticket with a metric and review date.

### Compact decision record template

```markdown
# Decision: [Short Title]

## Context & Constraints
What problem are we solving, and what deadlines or limits apply?

## Facts vs Assumptions
- **Fact:** Current p95 query latency is 1.8s at 2k req/min.
- **Assumption:** Adding a read replica cuts p95 under 300ms.

## Options
1. **Option A (Read replica):** Fast to ship (2 days); adds replica lag handling.
2. **Option B (Redis cache):** Sub-50ms reads; requires cache invalidation rules.

## Decision & Ownership (DACI)
- **Driver:** @backend-dev · **Approver:** @team-lead
- **Contributors:** @ops, @qa · **Informed:** @pm
- **Chosen:** Option A, with a 2-week latency review.
```

## Gotchas
- **Multiple Approvers:** Two Approvers means neither owns the risk and the decision stalls.
- **Treating assumptions as facts:** Validating a 2-day spike assumption before committing saves weeks of rework.
- **Escalating everything (or nothing):** Push local implementation choices down; escalate budget, customer commitments, legal exposure, and cross-team architecture.

## Production checklist
- [ ] Hard-to-reverse technical decisions have a written ADR (`process/adr`) with ≥ 2 options.
- [ ] Every major decision names **one Approver** and a decision deadline.
- [ ] Unvalidated assumptions are flagged for a time-boxed spike before full build.
- [ ] Decisions include a follow-up metric or review date to verify the outcome.
