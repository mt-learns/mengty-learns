## TL;DR
Great technical leads listen to **understand before replying**, write using the **7 Cs** (clear, concise, correct, complete, concrete, coherent, courteous), and replace vague "everything is fine" updates with **RAG status reports** that name the risk, decision needed, and recommended option.

## Why it exists
Most delivery surprises are communication failures, not coding failures. Engineers often report "90% done" while hiding an unconfirmed requirement, or hold status meetings that end without owners or deadlines.

## Mental model: Listen → Separate → Confirm
1. **Listen without rehearsing:** Do not draft your counter-argument while the other person is talking.
2. **Ask open questions:** *"Can you walk me through an example?"* or *"Which constraint matters most?"*
3. **Paraphrase back:** *"So the core issue is that finance needs a reconciled CSV before 09:00—did I get that right?"*
4. **Separate facts from assumptions:** Distinguish observed logs/metrics from guesses.
5. **Confirm next action:** End with one owner and one date.

## Do it: Templates you can reuse

### 1. Actionable RAG status update

```text
Status: YELLOW

Completed:
- Inventory adjustment API and audit log merged to staging.
- Automated integration tests passing.

In progress:
- Staging QA verification.

Risk / blocker:
- Product has not confirmed whether cancelled orders restore stock automatically.

Impact:
- Delays production release by 1 day per day of delay.

Decision needed:
- Confirm cancellation restock rule (Owner: PO, by Today 16:00).

Recommendation:
- Restore stock only after manager approval, matching our audit policy.

Next milestone / ETA:
- Staging sign-off: Thursday · Production release: Friday (if decided today).
```

### 2. Meeting decision summary

```markdown
# Decision Notes: [Topic] — [YYYY-MM-DD]

## Decisions
- [Decision] — Owner: [Role/Name]

## Actions
- [Action] — Owner: [Role/Name] — Due: [YYYY-MM-DD]

## Open risks
- [Risk] — Decision needed by: [YYYY-MM-DD]
```

## Gotchas
- **Raising a problem with zero options:** Escalating *"the third-party API is slow"* forces managers to guess. Always bring 1–2 options and your recommendation.
- **Meeting without written notes:** If a decision isn't written in the ticket or doc right after the call, each attendee leaves with a different memory.
- **Hiding Yellow/Red until the deadline:** Surfacing a Yellow risk three days early gives stakeholders options; surfacing it on launch day creates a crisis.

## Production checklist
- [ ] Routine status updates are sent asynchronously in writing (RAG format).
- [ ] Every escalation includes **Impact**, **Decision needed (owner + date)**, and **Recommendation**.
- [ ] Important meetings end with a 5-line **Decision & Action** summary posted in the shared channel/ticket.
- [ ] Unknowns are answered honestly with a follow-up timestamp.
