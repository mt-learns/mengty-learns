## TL;DR
A lead's job is not to hold every answer—it is to grow the team's **judgment, capability, and ownership**. Use **SBI + Next Step** (Situation, Behavior, Impact, Next Step) for specific, non-defensive feedback, and keep **1:1s** focused on blockers, coaching, and one clear growth goal rather than status updates.

## Why it exists
Vague praise (*"good job"*) and vague criticism (*"be more proactive"*) leave people guessing what to repeat or change. In Google's Project Oxygen findings, the top manager trait is being a **good coach**—someone who gives specific, timely feedback and creates regular opportunities to learn.

## Mental model: SBI + Next Step

| Step | What it covers | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation** | Exact meeting, PR, or day | Anchor to one concrete moment—never *"always"* or *"lately"*. |
| **Behavior** | What they said or did | Stick to what a camera or transcript would record; zero mind-reading. |
| **Impact** | Effect on users, team, or delivery | Explain *why* it mattered (faster review, blocked QA, lost trust). |
| **Next Step** | Agreed action or inquiry | Ask for their perspective (`Intent` in SBII) and agree on one concrete change. |

## Do it: Feedback and 1:1 rhythm

### 1. Constructive SBI example

```text
Situation: In yesterday's API design review for the checkout service,
Behavior:  you walked through the 400-line schema diff without summarizing the two breaking field renames first.
Impact:    Frontend and QA spent 20 minutes debugging assumptions before noticing the renames, which delayed sign-off.
Next step: What were you hoping to cover first? Next time, let's put breaking changes in a 3-line summary at the top of the PR.
```

### 2. High-signal 1:1 questions (biweekly or monthly)
Skip *"What's the status of ticket #42?"*—read the board before the meeting and ask:
- *"What slowed you down or caused unnecessary stress this week?"*
- *"What skill or system area do you want to stretch into next?"*
- *"Where did my review or feedback fall short in supporting you?"*
- *"What recent win are you proud of?"*

### 3. Everyday coaching moments
Turn normal engineering work into growth without extra meetings:
- **Code review:** Explain *why* a boundary or test matters, not just *"change this."*
- **Pairing:** Let the teammate drive the keyboard while you ask guiding questions.
- **Demos & rotations:** Rotate who presents sprint demos and who shadows on-call/release checklists.

## Gotchas
- **Turning 1:1s into status meetings:** If you spend 30 minutes reading Jira tickets aloud, neither growth nor trust happens.
- **Waiting for annual reviews:** Feedback delivered three months late cannot change yesterday's behavior. Deliver it within 24–48 hours while context is fresh.
- **Too many growth goals at once:** Agreeing on five goals guarantees none stick. Pick **one** realistic focus for the next 4–6 weeks.

## Production checklist
- [ ] Every teammate has a recurring, protected 1:1 (biweekly or monthly) that is never cancelled casually.
- [ ] Constructive feedback is delivered privately, promptly, and using **SBI** facts—never personality labels.
- [ ] Positive feedback is equally specific so teammates know what habits to repeat.
- [ ] Each teammate has **one** active growth goal followed up in 1:1s.
