## TL;DR
Teams rarely stall from lack of coding speed—they stall because people hold different mental models of the **goal, scope, constraints, or Definition of Done**. Start every initiative with a **one-page feature brief** that makes scope boundaries, testable acceptance criteria, and decision deadlines explicit.

## Why it exists
In Google's Project Aristotle research, **Structure & Clarity**—clear goals, roles, and execution plans—is one of the five pillars of effective teams. Without it, engineers either block on micro-questions ("Should cancelled orders restock automatically?") or guess silently, leading to painful rework during QA or demo.

## Mental model
Alignment is **front-loaded autonomy**:
- **Outcome** tells the team *why* the work matters (so they can make smart trade-offs).
- **Boundaries** (In scope / Out of scope + constraints) tell them *where* the guardrails sit.
- **Acceptance criteria + DoD** define *what finished looks like* before a single line of code is written.

## Do it: 8-step feature alignment checklist

1. **Define the measurable business outcome:** State the target change (e.g., *"Cut inventory transfer discrepancies by 50% across warehouses this quarter"*).
2. **Name the user and pain point:** Who struggles today, and what exact friction do they hit?
3. **Lock In-Scope and Out-of-Scope:** Write what ships now and what is explicitly deferred.
4. **Write testable acceptance criteria:** Use observable rules (permissions, state transitions, idempotency).
5. **Surface constraints:** Deadlines, latency budgets, compliance rules, legacy data quality, or team capacity.
6. **List risks and external dependencies:** Unconfirmed business rules, third-party APIs, UX sign-off, or migrations.
7. **Confirm Definition of Done:** Tests, review, staging verification, monitoring, and demo acceptance (`process/dor-dod-quality-gates`).
8. **Get explicit sign-off:** Ask PM, PO, QA, and ops to confirm the brief—do not rely on silent nods.

### Worked example: One-page feature brief

```markdown
# Feature Brief: Warehouse Stock Transfer

## Business outcome
Eliminate manual spreadsheet errors and give warehouse leads real-time visibility.

## User & pain point
Warehouse staff cannot verify in-transit stock before approving new transfers.

## In scope
- Create transfer request
- Manager approval step
- Dispatch and receive confirmation
- Immutable stock-movement audit log

## Out of scope (deferred)
- Offline mobile sync
- Cross-company billing
- Automated reorder forecasting

## Acceptance criteria
- Only users with `warehouse:approve` permission can approve a transfer.
- Stock cannot transition to `RECEIVED` before `DISPATCHED`.
- Every state change records actor, timestamp, and reason.
- Duplicate submit requests create at most one stock movement (idempotent).

## Key risks & decision deadline
- Approval threshold for high-value items is unconfirmed.
- **Decision needed from PO by Wednesday 15:00.**
```

## Gotchas
- **"We'll figure it out during sprint":** Unresolved business rules turn into mid-sprint blockers or wrong database schemas.
- **Missing "Out of scope":** Stakeholders assume deferred features are included unless you write down what is *not* being built.
- **Jargon in business briefs:** Keep outcome and acceptance criteria in plain domain language so non-engineers can spot gaps.

## Production checklist
- [ ] Every multi-day feature has a 1-page brief with **In scope** and **Out of scope**.
- [ ] Acceptance criteria cover happy paths, permissions, and failure/state rules.
- [ ] Unresolved questions have a named decision owner and deadline.
- [ ] The brief is linked from the ticket/epic and revisited at planning and demo.
