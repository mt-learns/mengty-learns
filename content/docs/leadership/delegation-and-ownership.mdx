## TL;DR
Delegate **outcomes, constraints, and decision boundaries**—not step-by-step instructions. Match your involvement to the engineer's readiness across four levels (**Direct → Guided → Consultative → Autonomous**), and never assign accountability without matching authority.

## Why it exists
Google's Project Oxygen research highlighted that effective managers **empower their team and avoid micromanaging**. When a lead delegates only tiny tasks ("create this DTO, write this SQL query"), they remain the single brain in the room, and engineers never build system-level judgment.

## Mental model: four levels of delegation

| Level | Lead involvement | Best fit | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Direct** | Lead defines method and checks closely | New hire or high-risk first-time task | Junior engineer fixing their first production bug |
| **2. Guided** | Owner proposes approach; lead reviews before build | Mid-level engineer stretching into design | Engineer designing a new service endpoint |
| **3. Consultative** | Owner decides after consulting lead/peers | Experienced engineer on cross-cutting work | Engineer choosing a caching or retry strategy |
| **4. Autonomous** | Owner owns decision, execution, and outcome | Senior engineer in an established domain | Senior engineer driving CI/CD reliability |

## Do it: 6-step outcome delegation

1. **Pick a meaningful outcome:** Frame the deliverable, not just an activity.
2. **Share the "why":** Explain the business context and who depends on it.
3. **State non-negotiable constraints:** Security, auditability, latency, or release dates.
4. **Define the decision boundary:** Clarify what the owner can decide alone vs what needs review.
5. **Agree on check-in points:** Set a risk-check date *before* the deadline (e.g., *"Flag blockers by Tuesday"*).
6. **Review and recognize:** Debrief what went well and credit the owner publicly.

### Weak vs strong delegation

```text
Weak:
"Build the stock-adjustment API."

Strong:
"You own delivering the stock-adjustment API to staging by Thursday.
It must enforce role permissions, write an immutable audit log, pass
unit and integration tests, and include OpenAPI docs for QA. You choose
the internal module structure; flag any schema blockers by Tuesday."
```

## Gotchas
- **Accountability without authority:** Asking someone to "own release quality" while denying them permission to block an untested PR sets them up to fail.
- **Stealing ownership back:** If you rewrite their PR yourself at the first imperfection, you teach them that *you* are still the real owner. Coach and pair instead.
- **Silent abdication:** "Autonomous" does not mean disappearing. Keep lightweight check-ins and remove organizational blockers.

## Production checklist
- [ ] Every delegated item has a clear **outcome**, **constraints**, and **risk-escalation date**.
- [ ] The owner has the repo, environment, and decision authority required to succeed.
- [ ] Ownership is visible in the issue tracker and team RACI (`process/raci`).
- [ ] You use delegation deliberately to grow people one level up, not just to offload chores.
