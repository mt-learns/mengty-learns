## TL;DR
The most durable senior engineers avoid the false choice between "pure coder" and "non-technical manager." Keep **one deep technical domain** (the vertical stroke) and build **working competence** across team leadership, project delivery, and product judgment (the horizontal stroke) through a sequenced 6-phase roadmap.

## Why it exists
Pure technical depth without delivery or product awareness leaves great engineers sidelined when roadmap priorities are set. Conversely, stepping completely away from code causes technical judgment to decay within two years. Combining both lets you remove the translation layer between engineering reality and business decisions.

## Mental model: Vertical depth + Horizontal breadth
- **Vertical stroke (Mastery):** Architecture, security, data, APIs, and production operations in one core domain. Depth earns your seat in technical reviews.
- **Horizontal stroke (Working competence):**
  - **Team leadership:** Mentoring, delegation, code-review culture, conflict resolution.
  - **Project management:** Scope, milestones, risk logs, honest status updates.
  - **Product ownership:** User value, acceptance criteria, outcome measurement, saying no with trade-offs.

## Do it: Six-phase roadmap (with readiness signals)

| Phase | Focus | Key artefact | Readiness signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Engineering depth** *(Mo 1–3)* | Consolidate vertical stroke | End-to-end owned design + durable ADR | Peers bring design questions to you early |
| **2. Technical lead** *(Mo 3–7)* | Multiply the team | Delegated outcome + improved team practice | PRs ship in your area without you coding them |
| **3. Project leadership** *(Mo 6–10)* | Defensible delivery | Charter, milestone plan, risk log, RAG updates | Written status trusted without follow-up calls |
| **4. Product capability** *(Mo 9–13)* | Build the right thing | User story + measured post-launch outcome | You said "no" in writing and it stuck |
| **5. Combined leadership** *(Mo 12–16)* | Hold all lenses at once | Cross-functional initiative led end to end | Execs and engineers both come to you directly |
| **6. Evidence portfolio** *(Mo 15–18)* | Assemble real proof | Outcome-framed portfolio + 360 feedback | Promotion panel already knows your artefacts |

### Start in week one on real work
Do not invent fake side projects to practice leadership. Pick **one real, funded workstream**, agree on a short personal charter with your manager, and produce one real artefact per week (an ADR, a properly scoped story, a written RAG update, and a delegated task).

## Gotchas
- **Skipping Phase 1:** Trying to lead delivery before peers trust your engineering judgment creates a lead nobody follows.
- **Trying to master all four tracks at once:** Working competence means *good enough to unblock and align*, not replacing a full-time PM or PO.
- **Collecting anecdotes instead of artefacts:** Save your ADRs, risk logs, and post-launch metrics as you go so promotion or role transitions rely on evidence, not memory.

## Production checklist
- [ ] You have one clear technical domain where you still review designs and production behavior.
- [ ] You have a written 1-paragraph growth charter agreed with your manager against a real workstream.
- [ ] Each phase's artefacts (ADR, charter, risk log, delegated outcome) live in shared team docs.
- [ ] You measure progress by **team independence** (can they ship without you?), not personal heroics.
