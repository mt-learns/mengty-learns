## 1. Intent

<Intent>
Becoming a Team Lead is a **role change, not a promotion for writing faster code**. You move from optimizing your own pull requests to multiplying the team across technical direction, delivery, people growth, stakeholders, and process.
</Intent>

## 2. Problem

Many new leads fail in their first months because they keep working like a Senior IC who also attends extra meetings. They grab the hardest tickets themselves, become a bottleneck in code review, and leave teammates without clear context or growth paths.

## 3. Solution

Treat the transition as a deliberate progression across four stages—**Feature Owner** → **Force Multiplier** → **Acting Lead** → **Team Lead**—while balancing five core areas:

1. **Technical:** architecture, ADRs, review standards, and technical risk.
2. **Delivery:** scope, dependencies, release readiness, and early risk flags.
3. **People:** 1:1s, coaching, onboarding, and healthy conflict resolution.
4. **Stakeholder:** clear written updates, options, and trade-offs for PM, PO, and ops.
5. **Process:** CI/CD improvements, runbooks, and blameless retrospectives.

## 4. Real-world analogy

<Analogy>
Think of moving from lead violinist to conductor. A great violinist plays their own part flawlessly; a conductor rarely plays an instrument on stage, yet ensures every section enters on time, hears each other, and delivers one coherent performance.
</Analogy>

## 6. Structure: IC vs Team Lead focus

| Dimension | Individual Contributor | Team Lead |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary unit of work** | My ticket and my PR | Team's sprint goal and production health |
| **Technical impact** | Writing clean modules | Setting standards, ADRs, and unblocking architecture |
| **When blocked** | Escalate or ask for context | Clarify scope, align stakeholders, remove obstacles |
| **Measure of a good week** | Features I shipped | Risks caught early, teammates unblocked, reliable release |

## 8. How to implement

<Steps>
  <Step title="Own one vertical slice end to end">
    Take one feature from requirements clarification through staging, release checklist, and production monitoring.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lead one technical decision">
    Write an Architecture Decision Record (`process/adr`) comparing at least two options and their trade-offs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run one stakeholder update">
    Send a concise Status / Risk / Decision-needed update after planning or demo so expectations stay aligned.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ship one team-wide improvement">
    Fix a recurring friction point—flaky CI, missing runbook, or unclear PR checklist—so everyone moves faster.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 10. Relations

- **Role clarity:** Use [RACI Matrix](/docs/process/raci) to separate who executes (`R`) from who signs off (`A`).
