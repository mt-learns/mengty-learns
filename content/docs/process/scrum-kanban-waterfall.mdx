## TL;DR
Pick **Kanban** for continuous ops, maintenance, or a 1–3 person team working with AI agents. Pick **Scrum** when a 4–9 person team needs a predictable sprint cadence for stakeholder demos. Pick **Shape Up** for 6-week product bets, and **Waterfall** only when regulations or hardware lock requirements upfront.

## Why it exists
Teams often copy enterprise ceremonies—daily standups for one developer, two-week sprint planning for an ops queue, or 40-page Waterfall specs for an exploratory product. Matching the delivery model to **requirement stability**, **team size**, and **interrupt rate** removes ceremony without losing predictability.

## Comparison table

| Dimension | Waterfall | Scrum (2020 Guide) | Kanban | Scrumban (Hybrid) | Shape Up (Basecamp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cadence** | Sequential phases (requirements → design → build → test → deploy) | Fixed-length Sprints (1–4 weeks, commonly 2 weeks) | Continuous flow, no required timeboxes | Continuous flow + optional planning/retro cadence | 6-week build cycles + 2-week cooldown |
| **Roles** | Project manager, analysts, devs, QA (often siloed) | Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers | No prescribed new roles; team owns the board | Existing team; optional rotating coordinator | Shapers (senior product/tech), small build teams (1–3) |
| **Artifacts** | Big upfront spec, Gantt schedule, sign-off gates | Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment | Visual board, WIP limits, explicit flow policies | Kanban board + lightweight prioritized backlog | Shaped pitches, betting table, hill charts |
| **Requirements** | Fixed upfront; late changes are costly | Changing; re-prioritized every Sprint | Changing; reprioritized whenever a slot opens | Changing; handles both planned features and interrupts | Fixed appetite (time), **variable scope** |
| **Best team size** | Large / multi-vendor regulated projects | 4–10 people (Scrum Guide recommends ≤ 10) | 1–12 people (great for 1–3) | 2–8 people | Small autonomous squads (2–3 per bet) |
| **Overhead** | High (upfront docs & phase gates) | Medium (4 events per Sprint) | Low (board + WIP limits) | Low–Medium | Low–Medium (upfront shaping, then autonomous) |

1. **Choose Kanban** if you are a solo developer, a 1–3 person team, or an ops/platform team handling unpredictable support and bugs alongside feature work.
2. **Choose Scrumban** if you like Kanban's pull flow and WIP limits, but still want a weekly planning check-in and a biweekly retrospective.
3. **Choose Scrum** if you have 4–9 people, a dedicated Product Owner, and stakeholders who expect a working demo every 2 weeks.
4. **Choose Shape Up** if 2-week sprints feel like a hamster wheel and you want 6 weeks of uninterrupted focus where scope flexes to hit the deadline.
5. **Choose Waterfall** only when external certification, hardware manufacturing, or fixed-bid contracts forbid iterative scope changes (even Royce's 1970 paper noted that pure non-iterative waterfall is risky without feedback loops).

## Minimum viable version (1–3 person team + AI agents)
Use a **3-column Kanban board** (`Ready` → `In Progress (WIP: 2)` → `Done`) plus a **weekly 20-minute review**:
- Keep `Ready` capped at 5–7 small, well-scoped tasks so AI agents or teammates can pick up work without ambiguity.
- Enforce **WIP ≤ 2** on `In Progress`—finish and merge one PR before starting another.
- Skip daily standups if you already communicate asynchronously in PRs and commit logs.

## When it's overkill
- Running 2-week Scrum ceremonies (Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective) for a **1-person project**—you end up scheduling meetings with yourself.
- Writing a multi-month Waterfall specification for a startup MVP where user feedback will invalidate half the assumptions in week one.
- Creating a Jira workflow with 11 statuses and mandatory story-point estimation when 10–15 lines in a markdown backlog would do.

## Gotchas
- **Cargo-cult Scrum:** Calling a weekly status meeting a "Sprint" while dumping unplanned mid-sprint requests onto the team gives you all the ceremony of Scrum with none of the focus.
- **Kanban without WIP limits:** A board with no WIP limit is just a sticky-note graveyard; work piles up in `In Progress` and nothing ships.
- **Misreading Royce (1970):** Winston Royce's paper is often cited as the origin of Waterfall, yet he explicitly warned that running the sequence once without iteration is "risky and invites failure."

## Production checklist
- [ ] Every active task has one clear owner and lives on a single visible board.
- [ ] `In Progress` has an explicit WIP limit (for example, `WIP ≤ 2` for a solo dev or `agents + 1`).
- [ ] The team agrees on when scope can change (anytime in Kanban vs next Sprint in Scrum).
- [ ] There is a lightweight weekly or biweekly pause to inspect what shipped and drop what isn't working.
