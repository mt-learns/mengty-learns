<Intent>
Shipping reliable software is a **closed loop**, not a one-way conveyor belt: **Discover** → **Plan & Design** → **Build & Review** → **CI/CD & Release** → **Monitor & Retro** → back to **Discover**. Small teams keep every phase lightweight so nothing stalls between idea and production.
</Intent>

## Problem
Without a shared map of the lifecycle, teams either **skip critical checks** (no problem validation, no threat model, no rollback verification) and ship incidents, or **copy enterprise bureaucracy** (15 mandatory sign-offs for a 20-line change) and stop shipping altogether.

## Solution
Treat the 15 stages of delivery as **5 macro-phases** with a feedback loop. Every stage has a job, and for a 1–3 person team (or a solo developer working with AI agents), each stage is explicitly marked **Necessary**, **Optional**, or **Skip** depending on risk.

<Analogy>
Think of cooking a new dish for a small café: you taste the ingredient idea first (**discovery**), jot down the recipe (**PRD/ADR**), check for allergens (**threat model**), cook and taste (**build/review/test**), plate and serve (**CI/CD/release/verify**), and watch whether diners finish the plate (**monitor/retro**) before tweaking tomorrow's menu.
</Analogy>

## Structure: all 15 stages for a small team

| # | Stage | What happens | Small team (1–3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Discovery** | Spot user pain, support tickets, or tech debt worth solving | **Necessary** (keep it to 15–30 min notes) |
| 2 | **Problem validation** | Confirm real users have the problem before writing code | **Necessary** for new features; **Skip** for obvious bugs |
| 3 | **PRD** | 1-page problem, goals/non-goals, requirements, success metric | **Necessary** for multi-day work; **Skip** for tiny fixes |
| 4 | **Stories & AC** | Small vertical slices with testable Given/When/Then criteria | **Necessary** (even 3 bullet points in an issue) |
| 5 | **Design / ADR** | Record irreversible or cross-cutting architecture choices | **Optional** (only when a choice is hard to reverse) |
| 6 | **Threat model** | 30-minute STRIDE check on any new trust boundary | **Necessary** for auth, uploads, APIs, external data; **Skip** for static copy |
| 7 | **Breakdown / estimation** | Split into ≤ 1-day tasks; T-shirt size or no estimates | **Optional** (slice small; skip story-point poker) |
| 8 | **Build** | Write code + tests in a focused branch or worktree | **Necessary** |
| 9 | **Review** | Self-review + peer/lead review for correctness & security | **Necessary** (even solo: self-review your own diff) |
| 10 | **Test** | Automated unit, integration, and a11y/lint checks | **Necessary** (automated in CI) |
| 11 | **CI/CD** | Deterministic build, lint, typecheck, test, security scan | **Necessary** |
| 12 | **Release** | Merge to `main` and deploy artifact or static bundle | **Necessary** |
| 13 | **Verify** | Post-deploy smoke test, headers check, or canary check | **Necessary** (30 seconds saves hours of silent outage) |
| 14 | **Monitor** | Logs, error rates, uptime, or Lighthouse/perf checks | **Necessary** |
| 15 | **Retro** | Short pause to turn friction into 1–2 concrete fixes | **Necessary** (biweekly or after every incident) |

## Minimum viable version (1–3 person team + AI agents)
For a small feature on a 1–3 person team:
1. Write a **half-page issue** (problem + non-goals + 3 Given/When/Then acceptance criteria).
2. Spend **10 minutes** asking: "Does this touch auth, user data, or an irreversible schema?" (if yes, write a short ADR + STRIDE check).
3. Build in a **1-day PR**, run `lint && typecheck && test && build`, self-review the diff, merge, verify the live URL, and jot down one retro note on Friday.

## When it's overkill
- Writing a full PRD, ADR, and STRIDE matrix for a **typo fix or CSS tweak**.
- Requiring a separate **QA sign-off phase** and **Change Advisory Board** meeting when you have automated CI tests and instant rollback.
