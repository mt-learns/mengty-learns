## TL;DR
For a small team, use **T-shirt sizes (S/M/L)** or **#NoEstimates** (slice tasks to ≤ 1–2 days and count throughput) instead of story-point math. Prioritize with **MoSCoW** or **RICE**, cap **WIP** so items finish faster, and budget **15–20%** for tech debt that hurts velocity.

## 1. Estimation: T-shirt sizes vs Story points vs No Estimates

| Approach | How it works | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T-shirt sizes** (`S / M / L / XL`) | Rough relative buckets (`S` ≤ half day, `M` 1–2 days, `L` split it!) | 1–5 person teams; fast triage | Letting `XL` tasks enter `In Progress` without splitting |
| **Story points** (`1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13`) | Relative complexity/effort/uncertainty scored in planning poker | 4–10 person Scrum teams tracking multi-sprint velocity | Turning points into hours (`1 pt = 4 hrs`) or comparing teams by velocity |
| **No Estimates** | Slice every task to roughly ≤ 1–2 days and track **items shipped per week** | Kanban teams, solo devs, AI-agent waves | Tasks that are secretly 2-week epics disguised as 1 ticket |

**Rule of thumb:** If a task is `L` or `> 2 days`, don't spend 20 minutes debating whether it's a `5` or an `8`—spend 5 minutes splitting it into two `S`/`M` slices.

## 2. Prioritization: RICE, MoSCoW, and Cost of Delay

- **MoSCoW** (best for scoping an MVP or release):
  - **Must have:** Non-negotiable for launch (e.g., static build + search).
  - **Should have:** High value, but launch isn't blocked without it.
  - **Could have:** Nice polish if time remains.
  - **Won't have (this time):** Explicitly deferred (prevents scope creep).
- **RICE** (best for comparing 10+ competing feature ideas):
  `RICE = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort`
  Score Reach (users/quarter), Impact (`0.25`–`3`), Confidence (`50%`–`100%`), and Effort (person-weeks) so loud opinions don't automatically win.
- **Cost of Delay (CoD):** Ask *"What does waiting one more week cost us?"* A security fix or broken onboarding flow has high urgency/CoD even if its RICE Reach looks small.

## 3. WIP limits (finish more by starting less)
By Little's Law in queuing theory:
`Average Cycle Time = WIP / Throughput`
When 1 developer juggles 6 open branches, nothing ships for two weeks and every branch accumulates merge conflicts. Capping **`In Progress` at WIP ≤ 2** (or `1 per person + 1 buffer for blocked PRs`) forces finishing and merging before starting new work.

## 4. Tech-debt budgeting
Not all tech debt needs fixing—ignore ugly code in a module you never touch. Pay down debt that **slows active work** or **creates reliability/security risk**:
- **Continuous (Boy Scout rule):** Leave touched files slightly cleaner (fix a missing test or type while you're already in that file).
- **Explicit budget (15–20%):** Dedicate every Friday afternoon, or 1 out of every 5 tasks, to upgrades, flaky-test fixes, and refactors.

## Minimum viable version (1–3 person team + AI agents)
- Size tasks with **S / M / L** (split anything `L` before starting).
- Prioritize with **P1 / P2 / P3** + **MoSCoW** for the current milestone.
- Cap **WIP ≤ 2** per developer (or 1 active branch per wave worker).
- Reserve **1 slot in 5** for tech debt and dependency updates.

## When it's overkill
- Running 90-minute Fibonacci planning poker for a 2-person team.
- Building a 40-row weighted RICE spreadsheet when you only have 4 tasks in the backlog.

## Gotchas
- **Using story points as a performance metric:** The moment management rewards higher velocity, teams inflate `3`-point tasks into `8`s.
- **Everything marked "Must have":** In MoSCoW, if 90% of the backlog is `Must`, you don't have prioritization—you have a wish list. Aim for ≤ 60% effort in `Must`.
- **Zero tech-debt budget:** "We'll clean it up after launch" turns into permanent slowdown and brittle deploys.

## Production checklist
- [ ] Every task entering `In Progress` is sliced to `S` or `M` (≤ 2 days).
- [ ] `In Progress` has an enforced WIP limit.
- [ ] Backlog uses explicit priorities (MoSCoW, RICE, or Cost of Delay) with a clear `Won't have` / deferred list.
- [ ] 15–20% of capacity is budgeted for high-interest tech debt and maintenance.
