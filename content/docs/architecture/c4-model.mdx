## TL;DR
The **C4 model** zooms into a system like Google Maps across four levels: **Context** (who uses it), **Container** (deployable apps & stores), **Component** (internal modules), and **Code** (classes). Below, we map **Mengty Learns** itself using C4.

## Intent
<Intent>
Draw architecture diagrams at a consistent level of abstraction so stakeholders, DevOps engineers, and developers each get the exact zoom level they need without a wall of boxes and arrows.
</Intent>

## Problem
Ad-hoc architecture diagrams mix "Customer", "Kubernetes Ingress", "PostgreSQL", and "`OrderValidator.ts`" on the same slide. Product managers get lost in class names, and engineers still don't know which process talks to which database.

## Solution
Simon Brown's **C4 model** defines four hierarchical zoom levels:
1. **Level 1 — System Context:** your system as one box, surrounded by its users and external systems.
2. **Level 2 — Containers:** zoom inside the system to show separately deployable apps, static bundles, APIs, and data stores. *(Note: in C4, "Container" means a deployable unit, not specifically Docker.)*
3. **Level 3 — Components:** zoom inside one Container to show its logical modules/packages.
4. **Level 4 — Code:** zoom inside one Component to show classes/functions (usually auto-generated or skipped).

## Real-world analogy
<Analogy>
Think of **Google Maps**: Level 1 is the **country view** (Context), Level 2 is the **city view** (Containers), Level 3 is the **neighborhood streets** (Components), and Level 4 is the **building floorplan** (Code). You never try to print street names on a world map.
</Analogy>

## When to use / when to avoid
<Checklist
  use={[
    'Onboarding new engineers—Levels 1 and 2 explain the whole system in 5 minutes.',
    'Architecture reviews and ADRs where mixing deployment boxes with class names causes confusion.',
  ]}
  avoid={[
    'Maintaining hand-drawn Level 4 (Code) diagrams—they go stale on the next refactor; read the IDE instead.',
  ]}
/>

## How to implement
<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Draw Level 1 (Context) first
    One box for your system, plus actors and external dependencies.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Draw Level 2 (Containers)
    Show every deployable app, worker, queue, and database, with protocols (`HTTPS`, `SQL`).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Add Level 3 (Components) only where needed
    Zoom into core services to show module boundaries; skip trivial CRUD containers.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Pros / Cons
<ProsCons
  pros={[
    'Clear, hierarchical vocabulary shared by product, ops, and engineering.',
    'Levels 1 and 2 change slowly, so diagrams stay accurate for months or years.',
  ]}
  cons={[
    'Level 4 diagrams rot quickly if drawn manually.',
    'The word "Container" occasionally confuses newcomers who think of Docker.',
  ]}
