<Intent>
**Layered** points dependencies **downward** toward the database. **Hexagonal**, **Onion**, and **Clean** all invert that bottom arrow so dependencies point **inward** toward the domain—they mainly differ in how they subdivide the core and name the boundaries.
</Intent>

## Comparison Table

| Dimension | Layered (N-Tier) | Hexagonal (Ports & Adapters) | Onion Architecture | Clean Architecture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Where business logic lives** | Business / Service layer | Inside the Hexagon (Domain + Use cases) | Concentric Domain Model & Domain Services rings | Entities (enterprise rules) + Use Cases (application rules) |
| **Dependency direction** | Top-down (`Presentation → Business → Data`) | Inward (`Adapters → Ports ← Core`) | Strictly inward toward Domain Model | Strictly inward (Dependency Rule) |
| **Testability** | Medium — often needs DB or repository mocks | High — swap Postgres adapter for InMemory adapter | High — domain & app rings have zero infra imports | High — use cases test with gateway fakes |
| **Typical folder layout** | `presentation/`, `business/`, `data/` | `core/ports/`, `core/domain/`, `adapters/` | `domain/`, `application/`, `infrastructure/`, `api/` | `entities/`, `use-cases/`, `adapters/`, `frameworks/` |
| **When to use** | Simple CRUD apps, small teams, fast prototypes | Apps with multiple entry points or external integrations | Rich DDD domains where domain model stability matters | Large systems with strict use-case boundaries & teams |

## The Order Service Takeaway

In TypeScript, **Hexagonal, Onion, and Clean share the exact same core mechanism**: the `Order` domain defines an interface (`OrderRepoPort` / `IOrderRepository` / `OrderGateway`), and Postgres implements it in an outer folder.

- Choose **Layered** when `OrderService` is mostly CRUD and extra interfaces are pure ceremony.
- Choose **Hexagonal** when you want clear **driving** (REST, CLI, test) vs **driven** (Postgres, Stripe, InMemory) boundaries.
- Choose **Onion** or **Clean** when your team wants explicit sub-layers inside the core (separating pure `Order` entity rules from `PlaceOrder` use-case orchestration).

<MyTake>
In practice, Hexagonal is the easiest inward-dependency style to explain and maintain in TypeScript projects. You get 90% of the benefits of Onion and Clean—fast in-memory tests and zero framework lock-in—without maintaining separate Presenter and Input/Output boundary DTOs for every endpoint.
</MyTake>
