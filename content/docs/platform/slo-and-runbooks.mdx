## TL;DR
Every production service needs **two artifacts before go-live**: an **SLO** (availability + latency SLIs with an error budget) and a **runbook** linked directly from every alert. Page on **SLO burn rate**, mitigate first (rollback or fail over) before root-causing, and run a blameless postmortem within 5 business days.

## Why it exists
Alerts like "CPU > 80%" or "Pod restarted once" train engineers to ignore pages. Meanwhile, an alert without a runbook leaves the 02:00 AM on-call engineer guessing which command checks database locks or how to roll back safely.

## Mental model

### 1. SLI → SLO → Error Budget
- **Availability SLI:** `good_requests / total_valid_requests` (excluding client 4xx errors where appropriate).
- **Latency SLI:** `requests_faster_than_threshold / total_valid_requests` (e.g. `<= 300ms` at p99).
- **30-day 99.9% SLO** = **43.2 minutes** of allowed downtime per 30 days. When the budget hits zero, freeze risky feature launches and focus on reliability work.

### 2. Multi-window burn-rate alerting
- **Fast burn (page immediately):** Consuming > 2% of the 30-day budget in 1 hour (`14.4x` burn rate over `1h` AND `5m`) → severe outage.
- **Slow burn (ticket / next-day):** Consuming > 5% of the budget in 6 hours (`6x` burn rate) or `1x–3x` over days → silent degradation.

### 3. Runbook anatomy (one page, copy-pasteable)
Every runbook linked via `annotations.runbook_url` should answer five things in under 60 seconds:
1. **Impact & severity** (what users feel, tier/SLA).
2. **Triage commands** (exact `kubectl` / PromQL / KQL queries to confirm the symptom).
3. **Mitigation steps** (rollback digest, drain node, scale replicas, toggle feature flag).
4. **Verification** (how to confirm the SLI recovered).
5. **Escalation** (who to page next if mitigation fails).

## Do it

Example Prometheus multi-window burn-rate alert linking to a runbook:

```yaml
# Multi-window fast-burn SLO alert (not run)
groups:
  - name: payment-slo
    rules:
      - alert: PaymentErrorBudgetFastBurn
        expr: |
          (
            job:http_errors:ratio_rate5m{service="payment"} > (14.4 * 0.001)
            and
            job:http_errors:ratio_rate1h{service="payment"} > (14.4 * 0.001)
          )
        for: 2m
        labels:
          severity: page
        annotations:
          summary: "Payment service burning 99.9% error budget at 14.4x"
          runbook_url: "https://runbooks.internal.example/payment/high-error-rate"
```

## Gotchas
- **100% SLO targets:** Demanding 100% leaves zero error budget for deployments, maintenance, or hardware failures—use 99.9% or 99.95% unless you have multi-region active-active funding.
- **IC doing the debugging:** In a major incident, if the Incident Commander also runs `kubectl` commands, nobody tracks the timeline, stakeholder updates, or escalation clocks.
- **Stale runbooks:** A runbook that references a retired namespace or old CLI flags fails during an incident; verify runbooks during quarterly game days.

## Production checklist
- [ ] Every tier-0/tier-1 service has written availability and latency SLIs/SLOs.
- [ ] Paging alerts use multi-window burn rates and include a valid `runbook_url`.
- [ ] Incident roles (IC, Ops, Comms, Scribe) and break-glass access rules are documented.
- [ ] Blameless postmortems happen within 5 business days with tracked action items.
